# Riverside Base — collection page

The eleventh collection page. One collection left, and the capacity question
now has an answer shaped like a single question rather than six.

---

## 1. The 30 × 35 / 30 × 25 conflict resolves itself

An earlier session pinned a disagreement: the catalogue gives Riverside Base
300 as **30 × 35 cm**, Jungle Flora's listing as **30 × 25 cm**. Page 25
settles which is *consistent*, with no measuring required:

| Model | Published | Printed capacity | A × B as a cylinder |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 30 × 35 cm | 17 L | **24.7 L** |
| 400 | 40 × 35 cm | 43 L | 44.0 L |
| 600 | 60 × 60 cm | 169 L | 169.6 L |

Two of the three rows are the plain cylinder volume of their own published
dimensions to within a litre. The third is not — 30 × 35 would hold half again
as much as the 17 L printed beside it.

The height that gives 17 L at 300 mm across is **240 mm**. The listing says
250. So the catalogue's 350 is the single figure in this collection that does
not reconcile, and the listing's is the one that does.

The page prints the catalogue table unchanged, shows the arithmetic, and asks
which is correct. It does not substitute the listing's figure.

---

## 2. …and it sharpens the Riverside Flow contradiction

This page demonstrates that the catalogue's capacity method **is** plain
cylinder volume, at least for two of these three rows.

Riverside Flow 600 carries the same published A and B as Riverside Base 600 —
60 cm and 60 cm — and a different capacity: **127 L against 169 L**. On the
Flow page *no* row follows the plain-cylinder method and all four follow a
different one (A × (B − 150)).

Both pages now carry a panel pointing at the other. Taken together with
Riverside Rise's repeated 31 L, the whole Riverside family reduces to **one
question for the client**: which convention were these volumes computed under,
and which rows need reissuing. That is a much better content request than three
separate ones.

---

## 3. The best photography in the catalogue

Pages 12 and 13 carry a full-page studio shot and **two real installation
photographs** — the CYBERCITY lobby with its neon sign and tan armchairs, and a
gallery corridor in the same building. These are genuine project photography,
not studio, and they are the strongest images in the whole supplied document.
They are the hero and second view here.

**Provenance note:** no extracted originals exist for pages 12 and 13 — only
the page renders — so those three images are cropped from the render itself at
about 770 px and resampled up. That is stated in the provenance file. If
higher-resolution originals exist, these three are the ones worth replacing.

Both isolated images ship with their own clipping mask (`p11-1` for `p11-0`,
`p11-3` for `p11-2`), so the group and the single product are pixel-exact
cutouts. That is now **three** pages where the catalogue's masks have turned
up.
