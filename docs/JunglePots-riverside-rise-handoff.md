# Riverside Rise — collection page

The tenth collection page. Two collections to go.

## 1. One capacity, three heights

Page 25 prints **31 L** against Riverside Rise 600, 800 and 1000 alike — three
cylinders of the same diameter and three different heights. The catalogue
analysis flagged this as *"inconsistent; hold from publication"*.

The page publishes the figure as printed and sets out both readings without
choosing between them:

| Model | Printed | A × (B − 150) as a cylinder |
|---|---|---|
| 600 | 31 L | 31.8 L |
| 800 | 31 L | 45.9 L |
| 1000 | 31 L | 60.0 L |

**A repeated row.** The right-hand column uses the reading that matches every
published Riverside Flow figure to within a litre. Under it the 600 holds
31.8 L, so 31 L is the 600's number and the two taller models carry it by
mistake.

**Or a fixed liner.** If all three take the same planting insert, 31 L is
correct throughout and the extra height is plinth rather than volume. No source
mentions a liner for this collection.

The page says plainly that the right-hand column relies on a reading which
contradicts the catalogue's own diagram — the contradiction set out in full on
the Riverside Flow page. Nothing is corrected or chosen.

**That reading now explains five published figures out of five** (four Flow
rows and the Rise 600) while contradicting the diagram that defines B. Whatever
the answer is, it is one question, not two, and it is worth putting to the
client as one.

## 2. Both isolated groups ship with their own clipping mask

`p10-1.jpg` is the alpha channel for `p10-0.jpg`, and `p10-5.jpg` for
`p10-4.jpg`. So the anthracite and cappuccino groups are both pixel-exact
cutouts rather than threshold guesses.

That is the second page where the catalogue's own masks have turned up (Cube
Shelf was the first). **Worth checking for on both remaining collections and on
the rebuild pass** — it removes the threshold problem entirely, which is what
cost the most time on Riverside Flow.

## 3. Two things the catalogue does not mention

**Four legs, not three.** Riverside Flow stands on three tubular legs;
Riverside Rise stands on four. The two collections are otherwise the same idea
at the same 150 mm leg height, and nothing in either source draws the
distinction. The stand view on this page shows it.

**The first non-grey finish.** Rise is the third collection photographed in
more than one colour, after Grand Wall and Riverside Flow, and the only one
shown in something other than a grey or a green: a beige close to Cappuccino
1014 from the four swatches. Neither source names it.

*(The Grand Wall page still says it is "the only collection photographed in
two finishes". That was true when it was written and is now wrong three times
over — one line to fix on the rebuild pass.)*
